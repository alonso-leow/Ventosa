# Motion design (K-motion)

Motion work is planned in writing first and built second. A plan says what is on screen, when, why, and what the audio does.

## Principles to design with
Weight (squash/stretch), anticipation before an action, staging (one clear focus), follow-through after it, ease in and out, curved paths, small secondary movement, timing as personality, a little exaggeration, believable forms, appeal. Use them as a checklist, not a style.

## Arc
Inside an act or a short: hook (first ~5 s), build, peak, resolve with the call to action. For long videos the three acts in `tone.md` come first.

## Time budgets
- Anything under 2 frames does not register.
- Readable text needs at least 9 frames; micro-feedback about 15 frames.
- Transitions 1-2 s; a scene with dense information 3-5 s.
- Change something on screen at least every 8-12 s.
- Exits run about two thirds as long as entrances. Stagger a group by 5-10 frames.

## Audio layers (relative loudness)
Music bed 40-60%, effects 60-80%, ambience 20-30%, voice 80-100%. Duck the bed 6-12 dB under effects and speech. Fade music, never hard-cut it. Silence before an impact is a tool.

## Effect families
Interface (whoosh, pop, click, swipe), emphasis (impact, boom, ding, chime), atmosphere (hum, texture, riser, tail).

## Plan format
Per video: overview (title, length, size, fps, style, mood); audio plan (bed, sync points, volume envelope, effects with times and purpose); per scene (time range, what is visible, animations with times, frame-level timing, audio, transition out, focus point); technical notes (palette hex, type, easing or spring choices, assets needed).

## Typical lengths
Micro feedback 1-3 s · intro 5-10 s · demo 15-30 s · feature piece 30-60 s · story 60-120 s · tutorial 2-5 min.

## Reject a plan that has
Easing chosen at random, everything bouncing, no clear hierarchy, audio that fights the picture, no room to breathe, mixed visual styles, or no arc.

## Gate before a plan is done
Every scene has a clear focus; timing follows the budgets; audio supports the picture; effects land on key moments; transitions have a reason; hook to resolve is visible; the plan is complete enough to build from; every element serves the story; pacing fits the content; the ending resolves.

## Transitions
Cut between related scenes; dissolve for time passing or mood shift; wipe for a new section; push/zoom to drill into detail; match cut when two shots rhyme visually.
